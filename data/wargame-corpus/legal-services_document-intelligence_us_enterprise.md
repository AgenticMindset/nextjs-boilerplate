## Key Players

- Harvey — AI platform built with/for large law firms and in-house legal teams, strong in firm-scale diligence, litigation, and drafting workflows; valued at roughly $11B after 2026 raises.
- Legora — Fast-growing legal AI competitor emphasizing multilingual, cross-border document work; valued near $5.5-5.6B and used by 1,000+ firms and in-house teams across ~50 markets.
- RelativityOne — Dominant eDiscovery/document-review platform with roughly 40% market share and a 200+ app ecosystem; the default enterprise litigation-document infrastructure layer.
- Everlaw — Cloud-native eDiscovery and document-review platform positioned as faster/simpler to adopt than Relativity, increasingly bundling generative-AI review at no extra cost.
- DISCO — AI-first eDiscovery challenger competing on transparent per-GB pricing and bundled agentic review features without legacy platform overhead.

## Positioning

The enterprise legal document-intelligence market is bifurcating into "generative legal AI" platforms (Harvey, Legora, CoCounsel) built around drafting, research, and contract analysis for law firms and GCs, and "eDiscovery/document-review infrastructure" platforms (Relativity, Everlaw, DISCO) built around litigation-scale document processing and review workflows. Harvey and Legora are locked in a capital-intensive arms race for large law firm and enterprise legal department logos, while Relativity's market dominance is now being tested by AI-native challengers offering simpler pricing and bundled AI review. Enterprise buyers increasingly expect generative AI (summarization, privilege review, drafting) to be included rather than sold as a paid add-on.

## Pricing Model

Enterprise document-intelligence contracts are typically annual platform licenses in the low-six to seven-figure range, historically driven by per-GB data-processing and per-user review fees for eDiscovery platforms; the 2025-2026 trend is toward bundling — Relativity has moved its aiR for Review/Privilege AI features into the standard RelativityOne package rather than charging separately, and DISCO now advertises one flat per-GB rate with no ingest fees. Harvey and Legora price enterprise/firm-wide deployments via custom annual contracts (often six-to-seven figures for large firms) rather than per-seat SaaS pricing, reflecting the strategic, firm-wide nature of these deals. Everlaw sits below full enterprise pricing (roughly low-to-mid five figures per month) but is increasingly competing for enterprise-tier legal department budgets as it adds AI credits for batch actions.

## Known Weaknesses

RelativityOne's scale and 200+ app ecosystem come with legacy per-GB pricing complexity and slower AI feature rollout compared to AI-native challengers, creating an opening that DISCO and Everlaw are actively exploiting with simpler, bundled pricing. Harvey and Legora, despite massive valuations, are still relatively young platforms without the decades of litigation-workflow depth that Relativity has, making them stronger in generative drafting/analysis than in full-scale eDiscovery/production workflows. Across the category, hallucination risk and the need for attorney sign-off on AI-generated output remain persistent trust gaps that slow enterprise-wide rollout regardless of vendor. The sheer capital intensity of the Harvey/Legora funding race also raises sustainability questions that risk-averse enterprise legal buyers factor into vendor-selection decisions.

## Distribution Channels

Enterprise legal document-intelligence sales run almost entirely through direct, relationship-driven enterprise sales to General Counsel offices and Big Law firm innovation/knowledge-management committees, with long procurement and security-review cycles (data residency, privilege protection, ISO/SOC compliance) common given the sensitivity of legal documents. Law firm partnerships and pilot programs are a major discovery channel — vendors court top-tier Am Law 100 firms as flagship reference customers, then use those relationships to cross-sell into the firms' corporate clients. Legal-tech conferences (Legalweek, ILTACON), bar association events, and legal-ops analyst coverage (e.g., a pending Relativity IPO drawing benchmark attention) heavily shape enterprise shortlists before any direct sales contact.

## Recent Moves

- In March 2026, Harvey raised $200M at an $11B valuation and Legora raised $550M at a $5.55B valuation within the same month, underscoring an intensifying capital arms race in legal AI.
- Relativity confidentially filed for an IPO in March 2026 at roughly a $4B valuation, signaling the first major legal-tech public listing since 2021 and setting a valuation benchmark for competitors.
- Relativity began bundling its aiR for Review and aiR for Privilege generative-AI products into the standard RelativityOne package rather than charging separately, matching a broader 2026 shift toward all-inclusive AI pricing.
- DISCO and Everlaw both pushed further into all-inclusive, AI-bundled pricing models (flat per-GB rates, included single-document AI actions) to differentiate from Relativity's legacy pricing structure.
